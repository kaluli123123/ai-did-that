## What is translated?

Each edition includes the introduction, five case summaries with human contributions and limitations, source links, submission questions, and a prefilled issue link in that language. The full research dossiers, detailed editorial guidance, license text, and external sources remain in their original language, usually English. A full-case link is explicitly labeled as English in translated editions.

## Translation status

These 28 translations were prepared with AI assistance on 2026-09-17. They have not received independent native-speaker review. English is the editorial source. A translation correction can be submitted from any edition. Chinese has separate simplified and traditional editions; this does not represent two disjoint populations. Punjabi here uses Gurmukhi, not Shahmukhi.

When changing a source claim, check the matching translated summaries and links. Update affected translations or clearly mark them as outdated before publishing an incompatible source change. Do not imply that a source-check date proves native-language review.

## Reach target and measurement

The requested target is access for 80% of people globally. **The currently published fact is 29 reading editions, not a verified 80% population coverage claim.** Global population, including proficient second-language readers, is the provisional planning denominator. Language audiences overlap; adding speaker totals double-counts multilingual people. Spoken ability, literacy, script, and access to the internet also differ.

No overlap-adjusted population coverage estimate has been verified for this exact language set. Useful starting points for a future audit include [the DICL linguistic connectivity dataset paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04692-8) and [CSA Research's Economic Atlas of Language](https://csa-research.com/l/interactive-tools/economic-atlas). The latter measures online audiences and economic opportunity, which cannot be substituted for global population without changing the target. These references provide measurement context, not certification that this project reaches 80%.
