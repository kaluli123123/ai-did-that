# Words inside a carbonized scroll

**Announced:** 2024-02-05 · **Category:** History · **System:** Vesuvius Challenge ink-detection models

**Evidence:** Project-reported result · **Sources checked:** 2026-09-17

## What happened

Youssef Nader, Luke Farritor, and Julian Schilliger won the 2023 Grand Prize, announced in 2024, for revealing writing across 15 partial columns inside a carbonized Herculaneum scroll. The artifact remained physically rolled. [1]

## What AI did

Machine-learning models detected ink in CT-derived surfaces prepared through virtual unwrapping. Their outputs exposed markings for scholars to interpret. [1]

## What humans did

Teams acquired scans, prepared and segmented surfaces, developed the models, and interpreted the writing. According to the organizers, papyrologists reviewed anonymized submissions and a technical team audited and reproduced the methods. [1]

## Evidence

1. [Vesuvius Challenge award announcement](https://scrollprize.org/grandprize): winning team, review procedure, recovered text, and links to code.

## Limits

This was partial recovery of one scroll, not the complete library. AI ink detection and scholarly transcription are distinct steps. The cited review was organized by the challenge; this repository has not reproduced the scans or models. [1]
