# A voice from brain signals

**Published:** 2025-03-31 · **Category:** Communication · **System:** Streaming brain-to-voice neuroprosthesis

**Evidence:** Published research · **Sources checked:** 2026-09-17

## What happened

A UC Berkeley and UC San Francisco team demonstrated streaming synthesized speech for a clinical-trial participant with severe paralysis. Her attempts to speak supplied the neural signals; the generated voice was personalized using recordings from before her injury. [1, 2]

## What AI did

Models decoded speech-related brain activity and generated audio continuously. This was a trained neural decoding and speech synthesis system, not a general-purpose chatbot. [1, 2]

## What humans did

The participant supplied training and evaluation attempts. Researchers developed the models, worked with the implanted recording hardware, prepared training targets, and evaluated the output. [2]

## Evidence

1. [Nature Neuroscience paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01905-6): research methods and evaluation. DOI: `10.1038/s41593-025-01905-6`.
2. [University of California account](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/brain-voice-neuroprosthesis-restores-naturalistic-speech): participant, training procedure, and voice personalization.

## Limits

The demonstration required an implanted interface and participant-specific training. It synthesized speech rather than restoring biological vocal function. It does not establish unrestricted thought-reading or universal clinical effectiveness. [1, 2]

The paper landing page presented an access redirect during this check; the university account was accessible. This repository has not independently reproduced the experiment.
